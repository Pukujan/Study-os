# N=1 Method Guardrails

Study OS begins with one participant because the immediate objective is to design and debug a personalized learning procedure, not to establish population efficacy.

## What N=1 can do well

- repeated measurement of one learner over time;
- compare performance across interventions within the same learner;
- discover stable individual bottlenecks;
- test whether an intervention effect replicates within the same subject;
- optimize an intervention for a specific learner;
- generate hypotheses for later participants/studies.

## What N=1 cannot establish by itself

- average effectiveness for other learners;
- demographic/population effects;
- universal learning preferences;
- superiority to all existing teaching methods;
- robust causal effects from one before/after event.

## Borrowed single-case principles

Study OS should borrow, where practical:

- repeated baseline observations;
- repeated outcome measurement;
- clearly labeled intervention phases;
- alternating treatments when comparable tasks exist;
- replication across problems/sessions;
- explicit documentation of confounds/deviations;
- preserving raw observations rather than only summaries;
- visual/time-series inspection of performance alongside model-derived summaries.

The What Works Clearinghouse maintains formal single-case design standards. v0.1 does not claim to meet those standards; they are used as methodological guidance.

## Suggested first design

For Sliding Window, begin exploratory and progress toward stronger control.

### Stage 1: naturalistic observation

Use normal tutoring. Record where confusion arises and which interventions appear useful.

Purpose: discover candidate transitions and representations.

### Stage 2: repeated baseline/probes

Use multiple matched problems to determine whether a suspected gap is stable.

### Stage 3: alternating interventions

Where matched tasks permit, alternate e.g.:

- static state trace + prediction;
- semantic pseudocode + reconstruction.

Do not compare interventions that teach different underlying content and call the result modality-specific.

### Stage 4: fading and transfer

Require independent work and changed-surface problems.

### Stage 5: delayed retention

Retest later without first reviewing the lesson.

## Data interpretation rule

Prefer conclusions like:

> Across four matched Sliding Window episodes, Subject 001 made fewer state-transition errors after prediction-based traces than after passive explanation, with the advantage persisting in two unaided transfer attempts.

Avoid conclusions like:

> Visual learning works better.

The first statement exposes scope, measurement, and uncertainty. The second hides them.
